## Research conclusions

- Adobe explicitly calls out sharpness/focus at 100%, exposure, white balance, noise, artifacts, over-editing/over-sharpening, saturation, dust spots, masking errors, chromatic aberration and quality problems for image submissions.
- Adobe explicitly requires AI content to be checked for accurate anatomy/proportions and correct labeling. The fictional people/property checkbox is a factual certification, not a replacement for missing releases.
- OCR, logo detection, object detection and vision-language models are useful screening instruments but do not independently prove legal status.
- Adobe's Known Restrictions is explicitly not all-inclusive; refreshing it is required and a no-match cannot produce a legal clearance result.
- VLM findings should be localized and reviewable. Recent research on fine-grained AI-image artifact detection motivates interpretable evidence, but it is not evidence of Adobe's internal moderation thresholds.
- This research pass does not implement catalogue similarity or similarity checks, per scope.
